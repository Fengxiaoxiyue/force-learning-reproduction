\section{Methods}

\subsection{Rate-network dynamics and architectures}
For the generator state $x\in\mathbf{R}^{N}$ and rates $r=\tanh(x)$, the Figure 1A implementation uses
\[
x(t+\Delta t)=(1-\Delta t)x(t)+\Delta t\,M r(t)+\Delta t\,w_f z(t),
\qquad z(t)=w^\top r(t).
\]
The sparse random recurrent matrix has connection probability $p$ and nonzero scale $g/\sqrt{pN}$. In Figure 1A only $w$ is learned. Figure 1B adds a feedback state $y$, rates $s=\tanh(y)$, generator-to-feedback weights $J^{FG}$, and feedback-to-generator weights $J^{GF}$. Figure 1C applies the readout-weight update to selected rows of $M$, so the desired pattern becomes internal network dynamics. The implementations are in \texttt{run\_force\_external.m}, \texttt{run\_force\_feedback\_network.m}, and \texttt{run\_force\_internal\_all2all.m}.

\subsection{FORCE/RLS update}
At a learning step the pre-update error is $e^-=w^\top r-f$. With $P(0)=I/\alpha$, the shared RLS update is
\[
k=Pr,\qquad c=(1+r^\top k)^{-1},\qquad
P\leftarrow P-kk^\top c,\qquad
w\leftarrow w-e^-kc.
\]
The update is isolated in \texttt{rls\_update.m}. Training leaves feedback loops closed and unclamped. Testing continues from the terminal training state with all weights fixed. We report mean absolute error (MAE), root-mean-square error where relevant, correlation, final readout norm, and task-specific accuracy.

\subsection{Targets and case assignment}
The exact Figure 2D target follows the publisher-supplied MATLAB example:
\[
f(t)=\frac{a}{1.5}\left[
\sin(\pi qt)+\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\pi qt)
+\frac{1}{6}\sin(3\pi qt)+\frac{1}{3}\sin(4\pi qt)\right],
\]
with $a=1.3$ and $q=1/60$. Figure 2H integrates the Lorenz system with $(\sigma,\beta,\rho)=(10,8/3,28)$. The triangle sequence, four-sine target, noisy-target protocol, Lorenz parameters, and fast/slow sine periods are numerical reconstructions from the paper or supplement. Figure 2E, 2G, and 2J use deterministic structural waveforms because complete numerical specifications were not published.

\subsection{Scaled experiments and data provenance}
The exact reference-scale Figure 2D run uses $N=1000$, $p=0.1$, $g=1.5$, $\alpha=1$, $\Delta t=0.1$, and 1440 simulated time units. The initial audit used $N=300$--500 for expensive multi-network cases, except Figure 6C ($N=750$). The recovery pass uses $N=1000$--2000 for periodic and feedback cases, the published $N=1200$, $p=0.8$, $\alpha=80$ Figure 7B scale, and the full 600,000 update steps for Supplement S1. Figure 7B uses a shared-$P$ low-rank internal update because the exact 800-row sparse RLS implementation would require roughly 6 GB for inverse-correlation matrices alone. These substitutions are recorded in each MAT-file configuration and are central to interpreting failures. Figure 8 uses CMU Graphics Lab trials \texttt{09\_02.amc} (running) and \texttt{08\_01.amc} (walking): root translation is removed, angles are unwrapped, smoothed with a five-frame moving average, interpolated tenfold, demeaned, and reduced/padded to 95 channels. The source permits research and commercial use but requests acknowledgment and prohibits direct resale \cite{cmu_mocap}.

\subsection{Reproducibility controls}
Every stochastic run fixes a MATLAB Twister seed. Unit tests cover target dimensions, RLS dimensions and symmetry, fixed-seed repeatability, delayed-history continuity, feedback/readout sparsity, multifunction dimensions, chaos statistics, stored-rate dimensions, AMC parsing, and finite non-RLS learning rates. Baseline artifacts are saved under \texttt{results/data} and \texttt{results/figures}; recovery artifacts are isolated under \texttt{results/recovery}. Zotero item \texttt{D2YQS7LK} was exported to \texttt{report/bibliography/zotero.bib}; its citation key is \texttt{sussillo\_generating\_2009}.

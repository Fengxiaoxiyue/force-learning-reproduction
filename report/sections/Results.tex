\subsection{Output generation and architecture benchmark}
Figure~\ref{fig:arch-benchmark} compares the exact four-sine target across the three architectures. Figure 1A gives the strongest autonomous result. Figure 1C also stabilizes the target. The scaled Figure 1B implementation does not: the paper used a generator with 20,000 units, so reducing it to 500 units changes both representational capacity and feedback-network statistics. This negative result is not evidence against Figure 1B at the published scale.

All baseline and recovery traces are retained.

\subsection{Dynamical diagnostics}
The Figure 3 analysis stores network rates and readout history, computes an SVD of centered rates, and reconstructs the output from the leading eight components. The leading components recover the output while the eigenvalue spectrum retains a long tail, matching the paper's qualitative account (main paper Figure 3).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figure3_pca.pdf}
\caption{PCA diagnostics for the four-sine task. Source: main paper Figure 3, approximately pp. 6--7.}
\label{fig:pca}
\end{figure}

The Figure 4 interpolation uses $\gamma z_{\mathrm{target}}+(1-\gamma)z$ during training, but always closes the loop with $z$ during testing. As the train/test feedback mismatch increases, stability falls. In the paired-seed sweep, 80\% of trials pass the fixed stability criterion at $\gamma=0$, compared with none for $\gamma\ge0.2$. Figure 5 shows the complementary gain effect: low-$g$ networks require large weights and do not meet the cycle-MAE threshold, while $g\ge1.25$ reaches it in one cycle; test RMSE falls sharply at the largest gains tested.

\subsection{Feedback, control, memory, and motion}
Of the Figure 6 variants, internal learning is robust at $N=750$ (test MAE 0.00550), whereas delayed nonlinear feedback and the separately scaled feedback network remain unstable after high-cost recovery. Figure 7B improves from the initial mean MAE 0.519 to 0.0747 at the published $N=1200$ scale after correcting the input and modified-row protocol. Figure 7D remains robust: four maintained signs are read correctly on 98.74\% of test samples after ON/OFF pulses.

\subsection{Supplementary analyses}
Supplementary Figure S1 replaces RLS by $w(t)=w(t-\Delta t)-\eta(t)e^-(t)r(t)$ and $\dot\eta=\eta[-\eta+|e|^{3/2}]$ in normalized units. The full reported duration of $60{,}000\tau$ is used at $N=300$, 500, and 1000. All outputs follow while learning remains active, but frozen weights drift across three seeds; the allowed $\gamma=2$ reduces the final learning rate without stabilizing the orbit. Supplementary Figure S2 is reproduced more convincingly: at $g=0.8$ a few effective eigenvalues move far from the pre-training disk, whereas at $g=1.5$ the two spectra mostly overlap.
